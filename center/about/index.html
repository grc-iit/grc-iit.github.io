About GRC

Computing is now limited less by how fast it calculates than by how fast it reaches data. GRC exists to push that limit back, from the memory-bounded speedup model to software that understands the data it stores.

A university-wide research center at Illinois Tech since 2023, continuing the Scalable Computing Software Laboratory founded in 1999.

Mission

GRC turns performance models into memory, storage and I/O software, builds that software with DOE national laboratories, and trains the students, researchers and engineers who carry it forward. Its current work extends these systems to AI agents that manage scientific data.

Origins

From a performance model in 1990 to a university-wide center in 2023.

Xian-He Sun founded the Scalable Computing Software (SCS) Laboratory at Illinois Tech in 1999, after founding the first SCS Laboratory at Louisiana State University. Its program grew from the memory-bounded speedup model he published with Lionel Ni in 1990, known in textbooks as the Sun-Ni law, and later from C-AMAT, a model of concurrent memory access published in IEEE Computer in 2014. With support from NSF and DOE, the lab carried these ideas into memory, I/O and storage systems.

As scientific data grew, the limit moved from computation to data access. The lab’s systems work, much of it architected by Anthony Kougkas, turned its models into released I/O software: Hermes, a multi-tiered I/O buffering system; LABIOS, whose label-based I/O design received the Karsten Schwan Best Paper Award at HPDC 2019; and ChronoLog, a distributed log store for activity data. That work brought partnerships with DOE national laboratories, a patent and an engineering team.

On April 23, 2023, the SCS Lab became the Gnosis Research Center, a university-wide center at Illinois Tech (opens in a new tab), with Sun as founding director and Kougkas as co-founder and executive director. Gnosis is Greek for knowledge.

Research today

Gnosis Research Center develops computing systems for data-intensive science. Its research spans memory systems architecture, parallel and distributed I/O, resource management, and agentic science and AI.

Memory systems architecture

Memory hierarchy modeling, cache management, and compile- and run-time tools for high-performance systems.

  • Hermes
  • Memory Access Pattern Obfuscation
  • UniMCC
  • All Memory systems architecture projects
Parallel and distributed I/O

I/O buffering, parallel file systems, and data movement across deep memory and storage hierarchies.

  • ChronoLog
  • LABIOS
  • Coeus
  • DeepIO
  • DTIO
  • All Parallel and distributed I/O projects
Resource management

Task scheduling, data placement, and fast data access for high-end computing.

  • MPI4AI
  • UniMCC
  • All Resource management projects
Agentic science and AI

Context engineering and agent coordination for autonomous discovery: the CLIO architecture within IOWarp, memory layers for multi-agent systems, and AI-driven scientific workflows.

  • IOWarp
  • DeepIO
  • MPI4AI
  • Portus
  • SPOTTER-AI
  • All Agentic science and AI projects

Funding and partners

GRC’s research is supported by the National Science Foundation (opens in a new tab) and the U.S. Department of Energy (opens in a new tab). The center works with DOE national laboratories and facilities, including Argonne, Lawrence Livermore, Oak Ridge, Pacific Northwest, Sandia and NERSC, and with university partners such as the University of Chicago. Industry can work with GRC through collaborative research, technology licensing or sponsored projects.
