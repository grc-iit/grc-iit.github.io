Doctoral research

Currently closed. Doctoral research in high-performance computing, memory systems, and distributed storage
ClosedExpress future interest (opens in a new tab)This role is closed. The form records future interest and does not replace an Illinois Tech Ph.D. application.

GRC is not currently recruiting doctoral researchers. The role involves high-performance computing, memory systems, computer architecture, and distributed storage. Research connects with Dr. Xian-He Sun, Dr. Anthony Kougkas, and Dr. Bogdan Nicolae.

Students build systems, evaluate them on research computing facilities, and contribute to research software developed with national laboratory collaborators.

Research Areas

Doctoral work connects four areas of computer systems research.

Memory-centric computing and architecture

Study memory hierarchies, processing-in-memory, and memory-driven system design

Distributed and parallel I/O systems

Design scalable I/O middleware for exascale systems

High-performance storage optimization

Develop caching, prefetching, and data placement methods

Data management for AI and machine learning

Study storage for deep-learning training and inference pipelines

Examples from the center’s project portfolio include Hermes, ChronoLog, and IOWarp.

Requirements

The closed role describes the following background:

  • Bachelor’s or Master’s degree in Computer Science, Computer Engineering, Electrical Engineering, or a closely related field
  • Strong programming skills in C/C++ and Python
  • Interest in systems research (operating systems, computer architecture, storage, or networks)
  • Strong analytical and problem-solving skills
  • Strong written and spoken English communication skills

About Illinois Tech & the Department of Computer Science

Illinois Tech’s Department of Computer Science offers B.S., M.S., and Ph.D. degrees in computer science. Its research spans high-performance computing, distributed systems, data science, and algorithms. The department connects students with Argonne National Laboratory, Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory, and local industry. The main campus is about 10 minutes from downtown Chicago.
