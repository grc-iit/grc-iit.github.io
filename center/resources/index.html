Computing at GRC

Local systems for building research software, studying performance, and testing I/O and networking.

Members only

Ares research cluster

Ares is GRC's local platform for systems development, performance studies, and I/O experiments. Its compute nodes combine three local storage tiers with shared storage and an Ethernet network that supports RDMA.

AccessArrange an account with the cluster administrator. Use Slurm to schedule compute workloads; the login node is for editing and job submission.

For students

Future opportunities

Research at GRC combines systems work, mentoring, and participation in the research community. Explore the pathways below and talk with a potential advisor about a project that fits your interests.

Research pathways

Doctoral research

Doctoral students investigate high-performance computing, storage systems, and data-intensive applications through GRC's active projects.

Research combines coursework, independent systems work, publication, and open-source software development. Students present results to the research community and collaborate with national laboratories through funded projects.

Research areas

Memory System Optimization

Research into heterogeneous memory management and AI-memory co-design for computing architectures.

  • Heterogeneous memory management
  • AI-memory co-design
Data-Intensive Computing

Research into data placement, workflow optimization, and performance modeling for data-intensive applications.

  • Data placement
  • Workflow optimization
  • Performance modeling
System Optimization

Research into resource scheduling, performance prediction, and anomaly detection for complex computing systems.

  • Resource scheduling
  • Performance prediction
  • Anomaly detection

Master's thesis research

Master's students pursue focused systems projects within active GRC research. Thesis work may involve profiling, prototyping, benchmarking, or extending a research system.

A thesis may develop into a conference or workshop paper. The scope and research goals are defined with an advisor.

Performance Analysis

Undergraduate research

Undergraduates contribute to benchmarking, research software, documentation, and data analysis while working with graduate researchers and faculty advisors.

  • Summer research when funding is available, including NSF REU supplements.
  • Department research projects for Illinois Tech students working with graduate researchers.
  • Senior design capstones in high-performance computing and storage.
  • Summer internships involving benchmarking and software development.

Advising and mentorship

Students work with one or two faculty advisors. Research faculty and research software engineers provide additional mentorship.

Weekly advising meetings and group research discussions help shape a project and review progress. Agentic AI workshops, practice talks, and career advising support work beyond the immediate research problem.

Funded projects and co-authored publications connect students with national laboratories, universities, and visiting researchers.
